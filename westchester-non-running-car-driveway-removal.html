How to Remove a Non-Running Car from a Westchester Driveway

A non-running car can remain in a driveway because the owner is unsure how it will be moved. The vehicle may have a dead battery, mechanical failure, collision damage or tires that no longer hold air. The best first step is to describe both the car and the driveway accurately.

Check Four Basic Movement Details

Before calling, observe the vehicle without attempting unsafe repairs. Be ready to explain whether it starts, shifts into neutral, rolls and steers, and whether the keys are available. Mention flat or missing tires, a locked steering wheel, damaged axles or parts that drag on the pavement. If an answer is unknown, say so instead of guessing.

Explain the Driveway Layout

Tell the buyer whether the vehicle faces the street or the house, whether other cars are parked around it and whether the approach is straight or narrow. Mention gates, fences, carports, overhanging trees, soft ground or a steep change in grade.

If another non-running vehicle blocks the car, discuss it before scheduling. The pickup provider can then determine whether the location is workable and what must be moved first.

Remove Personal Property

Check the glove compartment, center console, trunk, door pockets and under the seats. Remove toll devices, parking permits, garage remotes, documents, medication, tools and other personal items. Do not remove parts that were included when the vehicle was described for the offer. Disclose missing major components before the final amount is confirmed.

Confirm Paperwork and Payment Expectations

Explain the title status before an appointment is set. Ask which ownership documents and identification are required. Confirm the offer, the circumstances that could change it and whether towing is included for the planned location.

The guide explaining what happens on pickup day can help organize the final checklist.

Blocked-In Junk Car Pickup in The Hammocks: Planning Guide

An unwanted car may be easy to describe but difficult to reach. A vehicle can be blocked by household cars, positioned nose-first in a tight space or left with tires that no longer roll. These details should be discussed before the pickup time is chosen.

Identify What Blocks the Vehicle

Describe the parking arrangement. Is another car directly in front of it? Is the vehicle against a wall or fence? Can it leave in a straight line, or must it turn sharply? If another vehicle must be moved, confirm who has its keys and whether that person will be available.

Share photos that show the junk car, the space around it and the route to an open loading area. A close-up of damage helps with the offer, while a wider photo helps with access planning.

Check Community Access Before the Appointment

If the vehicle is inside a managed or gated property, ask whether a gate code, guard authorization, guest entry or property approval is needed. Find out whether commercial or tow vehicles have designated entrances or time restrictions. The resident should verify property rules directly instead of assuming access will be allowed.

The related gated-community pickup checklist covers more questions for controlled-access properties.

Describe the Condition That Affects Movement

Tell the buyer whether the car starts, shifts into neutral, rolls, steers and has keys. Mention flat tires, locked wheels, collision damage, missing suspension components or a vehicle that rests unusually low. Avoid pushing or towing the car with another personal vehicle; let the pickup provider decide what is feasible.

Prepare the Handoff

Remove personal items and ask what documents and identification are required. Confirm who is authorized to release the vehicle, the pickup address, contact number and any property-management instructions. Also confirm whether towing is included and what vehicle details the offer assumes.

If the location changes or another car cannot be moved as planned, contact the buyer before the appointment so the access plan can be reviewed.
